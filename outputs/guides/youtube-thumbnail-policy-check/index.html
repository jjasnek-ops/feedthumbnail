YOUTUBE THUMBNAIL SIZE · POLICY CHECK

Can YouTube turn off a custom thumbnail?

Yes, the current YouTube Help page says custom thumbnails may be turned off for certain search results when they are considered inappropriate for viewers. A correct YouTube thumbnail size, file format, or crop does not replace the content-policy check. Prepare the image locally, then review the official thumbnail-policy wording before assuming the upload is safe.

Official source: Add custom thumbnails on YouTube - YouTube Help, reviewed 2026-10-14.

THE CURRENT OFFICIAL WORDING

Can YouTube turn off custom thumbnails in search?

The current YouTube Help article has a section titled “Why are my custom thumbnails turned off?” Its answer is narrower than a general claim that every thumbnail will disappear. It says YouTube may turn off custom thumbnails for certain search results when they are considered inappropriate for viewers. The same section says custom thumbnail images must follow the Community Guidelines.

“YouTube may turn off custom thumbnails for certain search results when they're considered inappropriate for viewers.”

— YouTube Help, Add custom thumbnails on YouTube

This is a policy and presentation rule, not a pixel-size rule. The official page does not say that resizing an image, exporting it as a listed format, or using a particular canvas will make policy-sensitive content acceptable. It also does not define every possible search-result decision in that short answer, so this guide does not invent a more detailed enforcement test.

Size check passed ≠ policy check passed
WHAT THE POLICY SECTION ADDS

What content does the official thumbnail-policy section name?

The same Help page says all custom thumbnail images must follow the Community Guidelines. It names examples of prohibited content in the checked section: nudity or sexually provocative content, hate speech, violence, and harmful or dangerous content. The page also states that thumbnails may be rejected and that a strike may be issued when a thumbnail contains those categories.

The page describes stronger consequences for repeat offenses, including removal of custom-thumbnail privileges for a period or account termination. It also says that a creator receives an email and sees an alert in Channel Settings when a strike is issued, and that an appeal may restore a thumbnail if the appeal is approved and the thumbnail has not already been replaced. Those are the official page’s stated boundaries; they are not a substitute for reading the linked Community Guidelines.

A SAFER PRE-UPLOAD CHECK

How should you check a thumbnail before upload?

  1. Check the image locally. Open the thumbnail resizer or the local feed preview, choose the image, and inspect the crop and title placement.
  2. Record the file facts separately. Note the local input and export path you actually used. A file format or size result is not a policy approval.
  3. Read the current official policy section. Use the YouTube Help page and its Community Guidelines link instead of assuming that a clean-looking image is automatically acceptable.
  4. Look for the named risk categories. The checked page names nudity or sexually provocative content, hate speech, violence, and harmful or dangerous content. Do not turn those examples into a complete legal checklist.
  5. Keep the product response precise. If a thumbnail is rejected, turned off for certain search results, or associated with a strike, follow the current YouTube notice and appeal path. Do not promise restoration or a fixed display result.

This workflow combines the current official Help wording with reproducible first-party local behavior. It does not perform moderation, submit an image to YouTube, or predict a search-result decision.
